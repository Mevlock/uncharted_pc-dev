**Unofficial fork build.** The official port is [SuperMedo's uncharted_pc](https://github.com/Supermedo/uncharted_pc); please don't report problems with this build to him. Everything in 0.3.6.56 plus the changes below.

**Installing:** the launcher of 0.3.6.30 or newer offers this update itself; or unpack into a new folder and start `Uncharted.exe`.

> **A big thank you to [antikmewzik](https://github.com/antikmewzik)** for his keyboard, mouse and controller work
> ([uncharted_pc_controls_qol](https://github.com/antikmewzik/uncharted_pc_controls_qol)): the button prompts, the mouse in

## After updating
- **The shaders are prepared once more, more fully than usual, at the first start of each game.** The fix for the
  floating dust and snow (below) changes how some shaders are translated, so the shader cache of earlier versions is
  rebuilt. It happens once.

## Faster start
- **Uncharted 1 and 2 load their start-up files much faster.** While the game loads a big file at start-up (for
  example Uncharted 2's HUD, 25 MB), it waits in steps of 1 second for the graphics memory to be filled, three pieces
  at a time. On a PS4 the disc is slower than that, so the wait hardly happened; on a PC the file is read at once and
  the game sat through many of these 1-second steps. The step is now one PS4 frame (1/60 s). The loading order and
  everything the game loads stay the same.
- **Faster boot, for everyone:** between the end of the shader preparation and the title screen the screen runs at
  480 Hz, so the title's files, which load a few per frame, are done sooner. The normal frame rate is back as soon as
  the title shows. While the logo movie plays the normal rate runs, so the logos and their music play as before.
- **Skip intro logos** (launcher, Game) is back as its own option, off by default, as in earlier versions (your
  earlier choice is kept). Ticked, the logo movie is no longer played at all (instead of the old black stand-in
  movie), and the screen stays black until the title's picture is drawn (in Uncharted 2 the game's own fog-grey
  frames showed for about a second there).
- Measured from starting a game to its title screen on an RTX 5080 PC, EU 1.02, shaders already prepared, with
  *Skip intro logos* ticked: Uncharted 1 about 4.5 s sooner, Uncharted 2 about 4 s sooner (mostly the start-up
  files), Uncharted 3 about 1.2 s sooner (1.7 s -> 0.5 s after the shader preparation). With the logos playing (the
  default) much of the loading already happened behind the logo movie; there the title comes about 0.3 s sooner after
  the logos. The times on your PC depend on the drive, the CPU and the shader cache. Loading a level from the menu is
  not changed.

## Anti-aliasing reworked (DLSS, FSR, XeSS, TAA)
- **The stripes, dotted bands and "settling" pictures after loads and checkpoint restarts are fixed at their cause**
  (GeForce cards, all three games, game versions 1.00 and 1.02). Temporal anti-aliasing wants every frame drawn a
  fraction of a pixel off. Until now the port shifted the scene's draws itself and repaired, one by one, the passes
  that did not follow (shadows, decals, single panels), with extra workarounds for the fades after a load. Now the
  game's own camera carries the shift, so every pass that uses the camera follows by itself, and the old workarounds
  are switched off. Examples that were reported: the stripes for a few seconds at the start of Uncharted 2's ice cave,
  the dotted band in the haze after *Restart checkpoint* in Uncharted 3's desert.
- Radeon and Intel cards keep the earlier method for now (the new one has only been run on GeForce cards).
  `BB_PROJECTION_JITTER=1` in the launcher's Extra variables turns the new one on there, `BB_PROJECTION_JITTER=0`
  turns it off on a GeForce.
- **Native (DLAA and the other upscalers at Native) in Uncharted 2 and 3 now works on the scene before the game's depth
  of field, bloom and motion blur** instead of on the finished picture, so those effects are no longer smeared by the
  anti-aliasing. Uncharted 1 keeps the earlier order (it measured softer the new way).
- **TAA kept no history at all** and showed each shifted frame on its own, so a still picture shook. Fixed: on the
  Uncharted 3 street test view at 3440x1440 the flicker of a still picture went from 1.19 to 0.00 (how much
  each pixel varies over 60 frames, median over the picture, in brightness steps of 255).
- A camera that stands still now gives exactly zero motion, a camera cut resets the anti-aliasing's history also at
  Native, and the last pixel row and column of the scene are tidied before the upscaler (shimmer at the picture's
  edge).
- DLSS: one more level of texture detail (also at DLAA); with the launcher's DLSS model on *Default*, model M is used
  at Performance and model L at Ultra Performance, as NVIDIA recommends; DLSS frame generation gets the real camera
  and the area the HUD covers.
- Quality, Balanced and Performance still upscale the finished picture. Experimental:
  `BB_SCALED_BEFORE_POST=1` (Extra variables) upscales before the game's effects in those modes too (Uncharted 2
  and 3).

## Floating dust and snow
- **Dust motes and snow move as they should, in all three games.** Dust motes hung in clumps and sank, and snow fell
  in straight lines (for example the light shafts in Uncharted 2's chapter 9 and the snow at its start); now they
  drift and flutter. The particles' shader reads each mote's path from a table, and the port gave it only the first
  part of that table. It is the same shader in all three games.

## Uncharted 3: brightness at high resolutions
- **Uncharted 3 is darker above 1080p, and that is the fix.** The game's exposure meter only looked at the left part
  of the picture at render sizes wider than 2048 pixels, so scenes came out too bright: on the street test scene at
  3440x1440 the centre of the picture measured 134 (of 255) against 98 at the game's own 1920x1080; now 92. It meters
  the whole picture at every size. `BB_EXPOSURE_FIX=0` in the launcher's Extra variables brings the old, brighter
  picture back for comparison.

## HDR (new, off by default)
- **HDR10 output** (launcher, Display > HDR). Turn on *Use HDR* in the Windows display settings first; without it the
  game starts in SDR as before. *Paper white* sets the brightness of white in the game, the HUD and the menus; *Peak
  brightness* is your display's peak (Auto takes what Windows reports).
- **The games' own highlights:** with a render resolution set and DLSS, FSR 4, XeSS or TAA on a GeForce card, the
  scene's bright parts (sun, sky, fire, lamps) are shown brighter than white, from the game's own scene before it is
  squeezed into SDR. In all three games. Otherwise the game's SDR picture is shown in HDR10 at paper white.
- **Three tone mappings** (launcher, Display > HDR tone mapping; also live in the in-game menu, Insert, HDR section):
  *SDR + highlights* (the default: the game's SDR picture as it is, only the highlights brighter), *Game curve
  extended* (the game's own film curve carried straight on into HDR) and *RenoDX tone mapping*. The in-game menu also
  has the knee and highlight strength, an SDR | HDR split view for comparing, a vivid HUD option and the picture
  sliders.
- With HDR off nothing changes. HDR was tested on one HDR monitor (a QD-OLED at 3440x1440) with a GeForce card; please
  report how it looks on yours.

## Ultrawide
- **Cutscene videos switch to 16:9 without a delay.** Before, the switch to 16:9 came about half a second after a
  video started, as a visible jump. The port now sees the game start a video and switches at once; after the video the
  picture fills the screen again from the first gameplay frame.
- **Uncharted 1: the flashlight no longer has a hard edge on ultrawide screens** (also at FOV 100; and at 16:9 with a
  FOV above 100). The ultrawide view was widening the flashlight's own projection as well, so its soft light pattern
  was cut off by the narrower light cone. Light cameras are now left alone; mirrored reflection views are still
  widened. Found and fixed by [Bazovsky](https://github.com/Bazovsky) in
  [PR #33](https://github.com/Mevlock/uncharted_pc-dev/pull/33).
- **Wide HUD (optional):** launcher, Display > *HUD at wide screens*. The default is as before, the HUD in the middle
  16:9. *Wide* moves what the HUD shows in its left and right thirds (ammo, weapon, prompts) out to the screen edges,
  or to the edges of a 21:9 area on 32:9 screens. Menus and videos stay 16:9. All three games.

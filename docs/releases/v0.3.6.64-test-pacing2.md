**Installing:** unpack into a new folder and start `Uncharted.exe` (or over a copy of your install; your saves and settings in `user` are kept).

## What this is
- **0.3.6.64 with only the frame pacing added.** Each frame is shown at an even moment and the game's world moves by
  those same steps, which gives smoother motion at a capped frame rate. Nothing else from 0.3.6.59 or 0.3.6.60 is in
  it (no reserved CPU cores, no G-SYNC / FreeSync indicator, none of the other changes).
- **The wait no longer keeps the graphics card busy.** In 0.3.6.59 and 0.3.6.60 the graphics card itself waited for
  each frame's moment, which held GPU usage near 100% and cost frames on cards near their limit. Now the port waits
  on the CPU side and hands the frame to the graphics card just before its moment (well under a millisecond).
- **Back on by default, in a lighter form ("Balanced").** It needs no setting: start the game as usual. The launcher
  has a new **Frame pacing** setting for those who want the old, fuller wait or no pacing at all (see below).
- **Who it is for:** anyone who had lower FPS, high GPU usage, stutter or other problems with 0.3.6.59 or 0.3.6.60.
  Please check whether this build runs as well as 0.3.6.63 on your PC.

## Fixed since pacing1
- **The pacing no longer drifts or switches itself off for no reason.** pacing1 measured each frame as finished too
  late, so the delay before a frame was shown slowly grew, and on PCs with plenty of graphics power the pacing could
  wrongly decide the card was too slow and turn itself off for a while, then on again.
- **Graphics cards that cannot do the GPU-side wait** (some older or integrated ones) now simply play without the
  pacing, as with it turned off, instead of falling back to an older method that cost FPS. The log says so once.
- **No jump in the game's motion when the pacing stops or starts again**, for example around loads and cutscenes:
  pacing1 could move the world one step backwards or one step too far there.
- **Frame generation is even again when the pacing is not holding frames** (a busy graphics card): the generated
  frames were shown right after one another instead of spread out.
- **The rare one-frame glitches from data read back from the graphics card are fixed at their cause** (pacing1
  only guarded against them), and that data is ready more often.
- **Keyboard button pictures in the HUD and the Controls page** no longer stay on the PlayStation pictures when they
  first appear while a menu opens, closes or fades.
- **Frames are spaced more evenly when the graphics card is the limit.** With a busy card, a finished frame could get
  stuck behind the next frame's work on the card, so it was shown a little late and the one after it a little early.

## The Frame pacing setting
In the launcher, on the **Display** page under **Frame rate limit**:
- **Balanced** (the default): smoother motion, with the graphics card handed each frame just before its moment. Best
  for most PCs.
- **Smoothest (uses more GPU)**: the graphics card itself waits for each frame's moment, as in 0.3.6.59 and 0.3.6.60
  (with this build's fixes). The most even timing, but the card stays busier, which can cost frames on a card near its
  limit.
- **Off**: no pacing; each frame is shown as soon as it is ready, as in 0.3.6.63.

An entry for `BB_EVEN_RELEASE` or `BB_EVEN_PRESENT` in the **Environment** field on the **Advanced** page still wins over
the setting.

## What to report
- Your CPU and graphics card.
- Your FPS (and GPU usage, if you watch it) in the same spot with this build and with 0.3.6.63, and whether it feels
  smoother, the same or worse.
- Which **Frame pacing** choice you used, and whether another choice plays better.
- If something goes wrong (stutter, a crash, the picture freezing or flickering): the file `user/last_run.log` from
  that run (older runs are kept as `last_run.1.log`, `last_run.2.log` and so on).

## Also in this test build (small fixes from 0.3.6.59)
- Button pictures (keyboard, Xbox, Nintendo) are painted on their own thread, so prompts appearing no longer cause small hitches.
- Data the game reads back from the graphics card is handed over only once it is complete (rare one-frame glitches).
- Loading the driver's shader cache can no longer crash the start when memory is short.
